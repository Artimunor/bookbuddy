# Flutter vs. Kotlin Multiplatform for BookBuddy

**Recommendation within this pair: use Flutter for the Android and iPhone pilot, and keep the public booking pages in conventional HTML.** Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP) with Compose Multiplatform is a credible alternative, especially if the team already ships Kotlin and Swift. Its iOS UI is stable, not beta. Flutter's advantage here is the smaller amount of platform-specific integration a new team has to own, not a demonstrated speed advantage.

The [product brief](../idea/idea-points.md) makes the priorities unusual for a booking app: a foreman needs to see a worker's response quickly, a crew member needs to capture evidence with patchy connectivity, and an owner abroad needs to read translated updates without losing the original. Evaluate the stacks on those flows, not animation demos or nominal code-sharing percentages. Evidence cutoff and access date for undated live documentation, policies, repositories and package pages: **23 November 2026**. Scores are engineering judgements, not measurements. No packages were installed or devices benchmarked for this review.

## Performance on site

- **Flutter** compiles Dart ahead of time for mobile release and draws the UI using its own engine. Impeller precompiles shaders to reduce runtime shader compilation, uses Metal on iOS and defaults on Android API 29+ with a documented OpenGL fallback where needed. Native map views have composition trade-offs; the Android Hybrid Composition++ mode is experimental and has hardware/OS requirements, so don't assume it solves rendering on an older crew phone. Use the DevTools frame timeline on real hardware. [Architecture and compilation](https://docs.flutter.dev/resources/architectural-overview) · [Impeller](https://docs.flutter.dev/perf/impeller) · [Android platform views](https://docs.flutter.dev/platform-integration/android/platform-views) · [Performance profiling](https://docs.flutter.dev/perf/ui-performance)
- **KMP** compiles shared code to a native binary with Kotlin/Native on iOS; Compose uses Jetpack Compose on Android and its own Skia-based UI on iOS, with UIKit/SwiftUI interop. JetBrains' 1.8 benchmark compared its Compose sample with equivalent SwiftUI and reported comparable startup and scroll behaviour, with about 9 MB of extra app size in that sample. This is a vendor benchmark for one workload, not a Flutter comparison or a device-size forecast. [Kotlin/Native](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/native-overview.html) · [Compose and Jetpack Compose](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/multiplatform/compose-multiplatform-and-jetpack-compose.html) · [Skiko](https://github.com/JetBrains/skiko) · [JetBrains benchmark, May 2025](https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2025/05/compose-multiplatform-1-8-0-released-compose-multiplatform-for-ios-is-stable-and-production-ready/#performance-that-s-ready-for-production)

Neither stack has a demonstrated edge on this workload **[unverified]**. Compare cold launch to the first cached job, frame timing through a long photo/chat history, memory and app size, and time to recover an interrupted upload on a modest Android phone and the oldest supported iPhone. Profile release-like builds, not an emulator in debug mode. [Flutter build modes](https://docs.flutter.dev/testing/build-modes) · [Android Macrobenchmark](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/benchmarking/macrobenchmark-overview) · [Apple Instruments](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/instruments)

**Where field reliability comes from.** Both clients need a durable outbox with stable IDs, deduplicated submissions and reconnect reconciliation. Stage media to disk and use resumable transfer where supported. Android recommends WorkManager for deferrable transfer and a user-initiated data transfer job for eligible, user-triggered visible transfers. iOS background URLSession uploads must be file-backed; OS termination and user force-quit are different (force-quit cancels the transfers). Show queued/uploaded/failed state and resume or retry when the app returns. A saved offline acceptance is *pending*, not a confirmed crew replacement: the server decides the winner of a competing acceptance. Apple also says silent pushes can be throttled or dropped. Use push to prompt a check of the authoritative job state, not as proof of receipt or a substitute for acknowledgement. [Android transfer choices](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/data-transfer-options) · [Apple background transfers and file constraint](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/downloading-files-in-the-background) · [Apple force-quit behaviour](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/urlsessionconfiguration/background(withidentifier:)) · [Apple background notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)

**Both stores:** Apple [App Review Guideline 3.1.3(e)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#other-purchase-methods) requires a method other than IAP for physical goods/services consumed outside the app; Google [Play Payments policy §3](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en) excludes primarily physical goods/services from Play Billing. In-person repairs, classes or tours fit that distinction; don't generalize it to a later paid digital foreman subscription or online content. The platform decision does not clear a Spanish marketplace settlement model or provider terms **[unverified]**.

**Translated messages are product data, not UI localization.** Use a backend translation adapter with selectable provider; for example [Cloud Translation lists Arabic and other supported languages](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/languages) and documents [multi-regional endpoints](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/advanced/endpoints) and [data usage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/data-usage). Keep the original, source language and target language with each translation; cache by message revision and target language rather than translating on every read. Show the translated text and original together, set direction per text block, and keep the original usable offline or when translation fails. Check mixed Arabic/Latin text, numbers, names, addresses and technical terms with actual users; provider quality, dialect fit and data-processing approval for these messages remain **[unverified]**. Voice notes do not imply automatic transcription/translation. Safety-critical instructions need human confirmation, not just a translated rendering.

**Web boundary:** start with ordinary HTML for public discovery, booking and customer links. If the signed-in portal later mirrors enough of the mobile job workspace, consider reusing UI there; otherwise share the API contract, event definitions and locale glossary, not widgets. Do not count on browser versions of mobile push, biometrics, background transfers or maps having the same capabilities.

## OTA, testing and release operations

**OTA.** Flutter's third-party [Shorebird Code Push](https://docs.shorebird.dev/code-push) can patch Dart for an already shipped Shorebird build; native/plugin code, assets and Flutter engine upgrades require a store release. Shorebird's [FAQ](https://docs.shorebird.dev/code-push/faq#store-compliance) describes interpreted iOS patches and its reading of store policy, not blanket store approval. Apple [Review Guideline 2.5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#software-requirements) bars downloading/executing code that introduces or changes features/functionality; Apple's [Developer Program License Agreement §3.3.1(B)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/) has a conditional interpreted-code provision. Google [Device and Network Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379?hl=en) bars self-updates and externally downloaded executables such as DEX or `.so` files, with a narrow exception for code in an interpreter/VM with indirect access to Android APIs. Don't assume any mechanism authorizes a particular patch; BookBuddy's patch scope and exact SDK compatibility are **[unverified]**. No supported KMP-wide OTA patch path for compiled app code was established in this review; [KMP's documented release route](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/multiplatform/multiplatform-publish-apps.html) is normal platform distribution. In either case, ship reviewed binaries with [Play staged updates](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6346149?hl=en) and [Apple phased releases](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/update-your-app/release-a-version-update-in-phases); use server-side controls for already-shipped, disclosed functionality and data changes, not as a way around review.

**Testing.** Flutter offers [Dart unit, widget and integration tests](https://docs.flutter.dev/testing/overview), including [semantics and accessibility guideline tests](https://docs.flutter.dev/ui/accessibility/accessibility-testing). Its standard `integration_test` cannot drive OS permission dialogs or other native UI; cover those with native automation or an explicitly chosen tool such as [Patrol](https://patrol.leancode.co/documentation/native/overview). KMP has [`kotlin.test` in shared tests and platform source sets](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/multiplatform/multiplatform-run-tests.html) and [Compose UI tests](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/multiplatform/compose-test.html); the [v2 API landed in the 1.11 line, May/June 2026](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/multiplatform/whats-new-compose-111.html#compose-ui-tests-v2). Reserve Android instrumented tests and iOS XCTest/XCUITest for platform seams. Neither UI test suite substitutes for signed builds on real devices. Before committing to the pilot, exercise a foreman alert to replacement acknowledgement, airplane mode and reconnection without duplicate submissions, and photo/video upload after suspension and reopening. Include Arabic layout with maximum text size, TalkBack/VoiceOver, denied permissions, a cold link into a job and checkout in the provider sandbox.

## Scored summary

Fit for a small, greenfield mobile team with no assumed Dart/Kotlin advantage. **1** = poor fit; **3** = feasible with meaningful caveats; **5** = strong documented fit. Weighted points = weight × rating ÷ 5. The scoring gives field reliability and integration more weight than web UI reuse or OTA; it does not imply a measured productivity gap.

| Criterion | Weight | Flutter / 5 | KMP + Compose / 5 | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Maturity and sponsor commitment | 10 | 5 | 4 | Both supported; Flutter's shared mobile UI has a longer production history. Compose iOS is stable, but its newer tooling and component tiers still matter. |
| Mobile performance | 8 | 4 | 4 | Both plausible; comparative field-performance data is absent. |
| Native integrations | 12 | 4 | 3 | Flutter has more ready-made adapters for the specific pilot features; KMP can use native APIs but needs more OS-specific wiring. |
