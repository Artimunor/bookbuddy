# Idea material

Every point that makes up the company idea, including points the main document has condensed or left out. This is the raw idea record; market and competitor evidence lives in `../markets/` and `../competitors/`.

## Core concept

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| What it is | An app through which builders and other construction workers organise their work, run as a managed service |
| Generalisation | The same booking model extends to other booked services: massage, pilates sessions, sport coaches, vacation tours |
| Team-based services | Teams are often run by a foreman who speaks the workers' language and organises them. The app gives foremen features to manage their team and to find replacements quickly for workers who drop out at the last moment. This applies to all team-based services |
| Nature of the business | A simple, real-world business, not a complicated high-tech product |
| Technology | The app and what has to be built are straightforward, even simple |

## Problem

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Recognition | Asked on the street, about 19 of 20 people know the problem of organising construction workers |
| Geography | The problem exists everywhere, not in one country |
| Most affected | People who buy many houses, repair them and resell them, and deal with construction workers constantly |

## Thesis

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Real-world opportunity | There is much opportunity in the real world now, compared with purely internet businesses |
| AI | There is simple, low-hanging fruit in real-world services now with AI |
| Usefulness over craft | Highly crafted, complicated products risk being admired but unused; a simple useful product gets used |

## Starting and adoption

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Starting point | A start needs initial users. Without them, the only route is approaching workers on the street, which is slow and unwelcome |
| First users | Know about five workers and operate them free of charge at the beginning, so they start using it |
| Pitch | Show the app to workers you know: an app to organise their work |
| Contacts | A well-connected local contact is how it starts; without knowing a single worker it cannot be done |
| Hardest part | Signing up workers is not easy and needs local people |

## Customers and the high end

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Property investors | Investors who buy, renovate and resell houses deal with construction workers constantly, and some fund companies of this kind |
| Wealthy owners | Wealthy people already have access to many apps of this type and do not want to deal with workers directly |
| Luxury property sales | Villas often sell for €3–5 million. The luxury market is very saturated |
| How the high end sells | Instead of agents bringing buyers, sellers host the agents at events: cocktail parties with champagne, music and a glamorous setting, turning a sale into an experience |
| Consequence | Selling to this clientele needs a different approach and people who know wealthy customers and what they expect |

## Scaling

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Localisation | Each place needs a localised version |
| Law | Each place has its own laws the service must follow |
| Limits | These make the business harder to scale; one app does not serve every place as is |
| Open view | Whether it scales across markets is undecided |

## Feature ideas

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Work organisation | Providers organise their jobs in the app |
| Booking | Customers book providers; extends to sessions, coaching and tours |
| Team roster | Foreman keeps workers with skills, languages and availability |
| Job assignment | Foreman assigns workers to jobs and shifts |
| Confirmation | Workers confirm or decline assignments |
| Last-minute replacement | Find a replacement fast when a worker drops out: own team first, then a vetted pool |
| Multilingual communication | Customer, foreman and workers each use their own language |
| Customer view | Customer sees who is coming and when |
