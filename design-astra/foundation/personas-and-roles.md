# Personas and roles

The first release is for managed repairs and small renovations in one Costa Blanca catchment. Customers get one accountable contact, the named foreman, even when crews change. The local service can step in when the foreman cannot resolve a problem. This design follows the [product brief, 21 Nov 2026](../../docs/reports/2026-11-21-builders-company-idea.md) and the [idea notes](../../research/idea/idea-points.md). The profiles below are design hypotheses, not interview findings. Launch language mix, devices and field conditions are [unverified].

## Customer personas

| Persona | Goal and friction | Language | Device and situation to support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owner abroad | Get a local repair scoped and completed without travelling. Cannot inspect in person, manage access at short notice or tell whether a chat promise is a booking. Needs photos, written decisions and someone answerable. | May prefer English, French or any other language while the foreman writes in Spanish. Keep the original available to consult with a trusted person. | iPhone or Android, often checking briefly between other commitments. Abroad and possibly in another time zone: show site and local times together when different. Can use photos already on the device or explain by voice without standing at the property. |
| Local owner | Explain the repair quickly, plan around the visit and know who will arrive. Unsure how to describe an unfamiliar fault; needs to separate what was quoted from what was added. | May prefer Spanish, Valencian or another language; do not infer from street address or nationality. Can request and answer in their own words. | iPhone or Android, possibly outdoors taking pictures in glare, in a dead spot near the property or holding a key with a gloved hand. Save the draft and show clearly when it has actually been sent. |
| Property manager | Keep several properties and jobs straight, coordinate access and identify whose approval is needed. Phone calls and ad hoc inbox threads make audit and handover hard. | May read in Spanish and communicate with owners in other languages. Use their reading preference; show original wording on decisions. | Phone between properties, in bright light or a weak-signal building; may review at a desk on a phone or tablet. Needs property filters, at-a-glance status and the site's time on every visit. |
| Renovating investor | Track several small renovations, compare approved scope with proposed changes and collect evidence and receipts. Was the higher amount agreed? Is a delay on one property or several? | Can prefer a different language from both the foreman and the property manager. Keep prices, exclusions, identifiers and change decisions tied to their approved version. | iPhone or Android at a site walkthrough or when travelling; may inspect photos and decide later from another time zone. Needs a readable side-by-side change summary without relying on extensive typing. |

## Provider, crew and later personas

| Persona | Goal and friction | Language | Device and situation to support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foreman | Own the handoff, scope work, schedule and assign a crew, then find eligible cover if someone drops out. An acceptance in chat or an unanswered offer cannot count as staffing. | Often acts as language bridge. Choose a reading language and explicitly list languages usable for the work and briefing; a machine translation is not proof of comprehension. | One-handed Android or iPhone between jobs; also used in sun, noise, gloves and poor connectivity. Show what needs action today, the next unfilled shift and the last confirmed status. |
| Worker | Know which assignments are offers, when and where to arrive, who to ask and what must be understood before starting. Needs a simple way to decline or flag a problem without contacting the customer. | May share no language with the customer. Select UI and translation preferences independently; preserve the language actually used for a message, not a guessed nationality or skill. | Android or iPhone, sometimes older or low on battery, with gloves, glare or no signal on site. Read the assignment before departure, use large targets and distinguish locally saved check-in from accepted check-in. |
| Provider business admin | Keep named foremen accountable, grant appropriate access and see whether people and jobs are eligible to proceed. May be the owner-operator, not a staffed office. | May use Spanish for records and another language for team communication. Show verified record status and expiry in the viewer's language without machine translating legal identifiers. | Android or iPhone at a desk or between jobs, occasionally at a site. Prioritise handoffs, eligibility exceptions and invite status; avoid making the phone a raw document or credential viewer. |
| Later-category provider | Fill a massage slot, run a Pilates class, coach a session or run a tour. Manage capacity, late cancellation and, where relevant, an eligible host replacement. | Choose UI and working languages; the language advertised for a session is a property of that session, not inferred from the provider's profile. | Phone in a studio, reception area or outdoors on a tour. May have little time or intermittent reception. Needs a simple roster or capacity view, not construction quote stages. |
| Later-category customer | Compare a location, time, duration, service language and cancellation terms; book quickly and know how to attend. A visitor may lack local context and may change time zone before arrival. | Use the app in their preferred language while seeing the session's actual delivery language. Do not imply a class or tour is interpreted just because the listing is translated. | Android or iPhone planning abroad or outdoors on holiday, often on mobile data. Needs the venue's date and time, accessible directions and a booking that stays easy to find after confirmation. |

## Mobile roles and guardrails

A persona describes a need; a role describes what someone is authorised to do in a context. A context is a role plus, where relevant, a specific provider business. Inside that context, access is limited again by property, job or shift. A role switch changes what the account is acting as, not who is signed in.

| Role | Can do within scope | Must not get by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer | Request work, follow jobs on allowed properties and contact the foreman. Quote/change approval, sign-off, payment and managing other people's access are separately granted on the property. | No crew staffing, private crew discussion, eligibility documents or workers' personal contact details. Visible arrival names belong to the confirmed, customer-visible crew. |
| Foreman | Own named jobs, coordinate the crew, see relevant skills/languages and status, offer or confirm assignments and lead the briefing. Accept jobs and publish commercial scope/changes only when the business grants that authority. | No blanket admin powers or access to unrelated jobs, billing credentials or raw eligibility documents. Cannot approve their own commercial proposal as the customer. |
| Worker | See and respond to their own offers, view assigned shift details, communicate with the foreman and authorised thread participants, acknowledge instructions and record attendance/evidence. | No unrelated roster, customer billing, private property access on an unconfirmed offer, or someone else's acknowledgement or attendance. |
| Provider admin | Manage authorised memberships and job ownership within one business and see the minimum status needed for business and eligibility follow-up. | No automatic worker or foreman access, customer approvals, or automatic membership of job chats. Act as foreman/worker only by switching into an explicitly granted context. |

- A small-business owner may be **Provider admin + Foreman + Worker**. Assigning other people happens as Foreman; confirming or checking in for their own named shift happens as Worker. Joining the business, being eligible for a task and being assigned to it are three separate states.
- A property manager may be a **Customer** both at their own home and at managed properties. Switch property within Customer; permission to approve, pay or sign off does not transfer between properties or grant access to job history outside the delegation.
- A later-category customer reuses Customer; a solo provider reuses Provider admin and receives only the operational contexts they need. Team-led services reuse Foreman and Worker patterns with role names appropriate to the service. Construction vetting does not carry across categories.
- If the same account could write a quote and see it from a Customer context, the approval action remains unavailable to that person. Send it to another authorised approver rather than letting an account switch act as a second signatory. Label the role and property/business next to every consequential confirmation.

## Internal operations (not a mobile role)

The company's local operations/back-office team onboards providers, reviews eligibility, coordinates exceptions and helps with access, disputes and no-cover situations. Mobile users see the status and a route to contact the service, not the internal review queue, investigation tools, documents or an ops switcher. The company's contracting role, eligibility checks, document retention and commercial authority are [unverified] operating decisions, not delegated to a role picker.
